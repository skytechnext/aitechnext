§ 01

Executive Summary

AITechNext exists because SMEs are being sold AI in fragments — a chatbot here, a subscription there — while the systems that actually run their businesses stay untouched. We sell the whole arc: strategy, build, run, and enablement, anchored to the ERP where the work already lives.

Who we are. AITechNext is a specialist AI agency, launched as a subsidiary of TechNext, a Singapore-headquartered Odoo implementation partner operating across Southeast Asia. The parent company gives us what most new AI agencies lack on day one: an installed base of ERP clients, delivery discipline from years of implementation work, regional presence, and a shared back office. AITechNext adds the layer TechNext's clients are now asking for — applied AI and automation, delivered as production systems rather than experiments.

What we sell. A full-stack catalog of 142 services across 17 categories — from strategy and data foundations through generative AI, agentic automation, computer vision, and MLOps, to governance, enablement, and managed operations. Two flagship practices sharpen the catalog into repeatable revenue: AI automations on Odoo (our structural advantage) and AI for sales & marketing (the fastest-payback entry point for most SMEs).

Who we serve. SMEs with roughly 10–500 staff — companies large enough to feel process pain, too small to build an internal AI team. Our first hundred conversations already exist inside the parent company's Odoo client base and pipeline.

How we make money. A deliberate mix of one-off and recurring revenue: fixed-scope sprints and implementations create the relationship; managed AI operations, care plans, fractional-CAIO retainers, and per-usage pricing compound it. The commercial goal is simple — every project should hand off into a recurring contract. Twelve distinct commercial models and their billing mechanics are set out in §12.

Why now. Model capability has outrun SME adoption; the constraint is no longer technology but integration and trust. Regional digitalization mandates (e-invoicing, grant-funded ERP adoption) are pushing SMEs onto exactly the systems we automate. And the window in which "Odoo partner who is also a credible AI engineering firm" is a rare combination will not stay open long.

§ 04

The SME AI Gap

Most companies don't have an AI strategy. They have scattered tools, isolated experiments, and a handful of people quietly figuring it out alone. That gap — between access to AI and an operating model built on it — is our entire business.

Fracture 01

Leadership isn't aligned

No shared view of where AI belongs in the business — so every initiative is someone's side project, funded by enthusiasm and killed by the next budget review.

Fracture 02

Teams use AI inconsistently

One salesperson drafts with ChatGPT, another doesn't; output quality, tone, and data handling vary person by person, with no floor and no compounding.

Fracture 03

Tools bought without architecture

Point subscriptions accumulate — a chatbot, a transcriber, a copywriter — none sharing context, none connected to the systems where work actually finishes.

Fracture 04

Company knowledge stays trapped

The knowledge that matters lives in inboxes, chat threads, folders, and the founder's head. The AI tools the team uses can't see any of it — so their output is generic.

Fracture 05

Pilots never become infrastructure

The demo impresses, the pilot runs for a month, then it quietly dies — no owner, no monitoring, no integration, no budget line. Nothing changes how the business runs.

Fracture 06

Projects shipped without a roadmap

The one-off automations that do survive were built in isolation — so they can't be extended, don't share data, and become tomorrow's legacy debt.

The problem isn't access to AI. It's knowing what to build, how it should fit together, and how the organization has to change around it.

The thesis this firm is built on

Each fracture is individually survivable; together they form a ceiling. The SME ends up paying for AI three times — subscriptions nobody governs, hours lost to inconsistent usage, and opportunities missed while pilots stall — without ever getting the compounding returns that make AI transformative.

Closing the gap takes all four motions at once. Strategy without engineering produces slideware. Engineering without strategy produces orphaned automations. Both without enablement produce dependency on the vendor; all three without governance produce risk the owner discovers at the worst moment. That is why the catalog in §05 spans the full arc, and why we sell the arc rather than fragments of it.

The offer, plainly

We turn AI from a pile of tools into how the company runs.

Strategy set with leadership. Systems engineered into the ERP and the channels the team already uses. Capability built inside the organization — so the value compounds after we step back, and the client keeps choosing to stay for what's next, not because they're locked in.

Delivery Methodology

One method for every engagement, tuned by size: prove value inside 30 days, ship to production — never to a demo folder — and hand off into managed operations. The method is also our margin: repeatability is what makes fixed-fee pricing safe.

Non-negotiables of the method

Baseline first
No build starts until the current process is measured — hours, error rates, cycle times. If we can't measure it, we don't automate it; the baseline is what makes every later ROI claim defensible.
Evals as acceptance
Every AI behavior ships with an evaluation suite agreed at Gate 2. Acceptance is "the eval passes on production data," not "the demo looked right." Evals re-run on every change, forever.
Human-in-the-loop by default
New automations start in draft/approve mode; autonomy is earned by measured accuracy over time, with thresholds the client controls. Trust is dialled up, never assumed.
Client owns everything
Code in the client's repos, automations in their n8n, data in their systems, credentials in their vault. Our recurring revenue must be earned by value, never by hostage-taking.
Weekly shipping cadence
Something demonstrable moves to the client's environment every week of every engagement — momentum is a deliverable, and silence is where projects go to die.
Handover as a deliverable
Every project closes with runbooks, architecture notes, recorded walkthroughs, and a trained owner on the client side — quality-gated like any other deliverable.

Competitive Landscape

Seven archetypes compete for the SME AI budget. None of them holds our exact ground — AI engineering depth and ERP process depth and SME pricing and regional presence — but each wins somewhere, and honesty about where is how we position against them.

The seven archetypes

Archetype 01

Global boutique AI studios

e.g. CustomAI Studio-class firms in the US/EU. Excellent strategy-plus-engineering story, strong content brands, premium pricing (US$50k+ entries) aimed at funded mid-market. Their gap: little SEA presence, no ERP practice, rates 3–5× ours. We copy their playbook's shape, undercut its price, and out-integrate it.

Archetype 02

Big-4 & global SIs

Accenture, Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG regional AI practices. Own the enterprise conversation, unbeatable for board cover and scale. Their gap: engagement minimums and timelines that structurally exclude SMEs; delivery via rotating juniors. We don't fight them — we serve the 97% of companies they can't profitably touch.

Archetype 03

Regional dev shops & software houses

SEA app/web agencies rebranding toward AI. Real engineers, local presence, competitive rates. Their gap: project-in, project-out culture — no strategy layer, no eval discipline, no managed operations, shallow business-process fluency. We win on the arc: they build things; we change how companies run.

Archetype 04

Automation agencies & solo operators

The n8n/Make/Zapier boutique wave. Fast, cheap, energetic; strong on social proof. Their gap: fragile builds without governance, monitoring, or data discipline; thin bench; nothing survives the founder's holiday. We win the moment a client asks "what happens when it breaks?" — and we inherit their orphaned builds.

Archetype 05

Odoo partners adding AI

Regional Odoo implementers bolting on chatbots. The closest structural threat — they share our ERP substrate and client type. Their gap: AI as a feature, not a practice: no LLM engineering bench, no eval/LLMOps capability, no AI governance offer. We win on depth today — and sell them white-label capacity (12.8) so their pipeline becomes ours either way.

Archetype 06

Freelancers & marketplaces

Upwork/Fiverr AI builders. Cheapest possible entry; fine for experiments. Their gap: no continuity, no liability, no security posture, no team. We lose the price-only buyer to them happily — and meet that buyer again a year later, with a mess to rescue (a standing entry in our pipeline).

Archetype 07

SaaS point tools & DIY

Intercom Fin, Tidio, Jasper, Clay-class tools; or an internal hire. Right answer for single, generic jobs — we say so in build-vs-buy (01.5), which is exactly why our recommendation is trusted. Their gap: tools don't integrate themselves, and one internal hire can't span strategy-to-ops. We orchestrate tools rather than compete with each one — and one salary buys our whole bench.

The real competitor

Doing nothing

Most lost deals lose to inertia, not a rival. The counter is the §08 method itself: a measured baseline, a 30-day first win, and an entry price under the owner's pain threshold. We sell the smallest step that proves the direction — because the status quo is the incumbent.

Billing mechanics & commercial policy

Fixed projects bill 40% on start, 40% at pilot gate, 20% at acceptance; packages under US$10k bill 50/50. Nothing ships to production with an unpaid start invoice.

Scope changes are welcomed, priced, and written — a one-page change order within 48 hours. The gate structure (§08) exists precisely so change never becomes dispute.

Pass-throughs
Model/API and infrastructure costs pass through at cost +15% handling, or run in the client's own accounts (preferred) — keeping our pricing clean of token-price risk either way.
Outcome guardrails
Model 9 requires: metric measurable in client systems, agreed baseline window, quarterly true-up, bonus cap ≤ 2× base, and a floor fee covering cost. If any leg fails, we quote model 1/2 instead.

Discounts trade for commitment (annual prepay, multi-phase signature, case-study rights, referenceability) — never for pressure. List price integrity is a strategic asset; the parent-client launch discount (−15%, first 10 engagements) is time-boxed and named.
Payment terms
Net 14 on projects, net 7 on recurring; grant-funded engagements (PSG/EDG-class) follow scheme disbursement rules with the client bridging. Late accounts pause change-work before they pause monitoring — we never let a payment dispute break production.
